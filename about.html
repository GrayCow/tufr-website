Our story

Built From Scratch

Trinity University Formula Racing is a student-run team that designs, builds, and races a formula-style car — starting, as it turns out, with a snowmobile engine and a lot of stubbornness.

The competition

What Is FSAE?

Formula SAE is a student engineering competition run by SAE International. Every year, university teams from around the world design, build, and race a small open-wheel car — the kind of car a weekend autocross racer might drive.

Teams are judged on far more than speed. At competition, the car is scored in static events (engineering design, cost analysis, and a business presentation) and dynamic events (acceleration, skidpad, autocross, and endurance). The best car on paper still has to survive the track.

The organization

What Is TUFR?

TUFR is Trinity's Formula SAE chapter: a non-profit, student-run engineering organization open to every major and every year. Engineers design the chassis, powertrain, aerodynamics, and electronics. Business students run finances, sponsorships, and outreach — because a race team is also a small company.

No prior experience needed. Most of what our members know about building a race car, they learned by building one.

Team history

The Journey So Far

2023–24Founding season

It started with a snowmobile.

A small group of students with no race car, no shop, and no budget decided Trinity should have a Formula SAE team. Their starting point was a snowmobile: its engine and drivetrain became the seed of the project, and the team's first real lesson in what it takes to turn a machine built for snow into one built for pavement.

2024–25Car 1 development

From idea to hardware.

Sub-teams formed, the team bought its first dedicated engine, and Car 1 took shape. Weekly meetings turned into design reviews, and a loose group of friends became an organization with leads, budgets, and a sponsorship packet.

2025–26Car 2 — now

Building on what we learned.

With Car 1's lessons in hand, the team is designing Car 2 with a bigger roster, better processes, and higher goals.

Why we do it

Our Mission

To give Trinity students a hands-on place to learn engineering by building something real.

We believe the best way to learn is to be responsible for a result. We build engineers who can design, manufacture, manage a budget, and work as a team — and we build a car worth being proud of.
